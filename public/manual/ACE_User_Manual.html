Section 01

Welcome

What ACE Presenter is, and what it does for your service.

ACE Presenter

01 · Welcome

ACE Presenter puts your song lyrics, scripture, sermon graphics, and media on the screens your congregation sees — and it can follow the service for you, listening to the room and moving to the right slide as songs are sung and verses are read.

Most presentation software makes one person click through every slide, watching the worship leader and the screen at the same time. ACE is built around a different idea: the computer should keep up with the service, so you can work by exception — stepping in to override, not to keep pace. When a verse is sung, ACE finds it. When the preacher says “turn to Romans eight,” ACE puts Romans 8 up. You stay in control; you just stop chasing.

This manual covers everything ACE does, from your first launch to multi-screen production. You don’t need to read it end to end. New operators should read Getting Started, Your First Service, and The Dashboard, then come back for the rest when you need it.

One manual, two platforms

ACE runs on Mac and Windows, and they are the same program with the same ideas. Where they genuinely differ, you’ll see one of these:

Mac only. This feature isn’t in the Windows version yet.

Windows only. This is specific to the Windows version.

Works differently here. Both have it; the details vary.

Everywhere else, what’s written applies to both.

A word on keys

Mac keys are written first, Windows second: ⌘S / Ctrl+S. Where a manual says “right-click,” a Mac trackpad two-finger tap does the same.

What ACE does

  • Presents lyrics, Bible verses, sermon slides, images, and video to one or more screens — audience displays, stage monitors, lobby screens.
  • Follows the service automatically. It listens and moves to the right lyric or scripture as it’s sung or spoken.
  • Designs the look with a full theme editor — type, colour, gradients, images, outlines, shadows, entrance animations — and recalls whole scenes in one click.
  • Maps the room. Assign every output to a display and save the arrangement as a venue you can reload.
  • Records the sermon and can turn the transcript into notes.
  • Streams and feeds video to a broadcast, and takes a camera in as a background layer.
  • Takes a phone as a remote so you can run the service from anywhere in the room.

Who this is for

Written for the person running the screens on a Sunday — not for engineers. If you can build a slide deck, you can run ACE. The harder production features are here when you want them and stay out of the way when you don’t.

Section 02

Getting Started

From download to a working dashboard in a few minutes.

ACE Presenter

02 · Getting Started

What you need

A computer. On Mac, macOS 14 or later; an Apple Silicon Mac (M1 or newer) listens noticeably faster than an Intel one. On Windows, Windows 10 or later.

A second screen for what the congregation sees — HDMI, USB-C/DisplayPort, or a capture device. You can also run ACE entirely on one screen while you build and rehearse; it previews every output inside the app.

A microphone, if you want ACE to follow the service or record the sermon. The computer’s built-in mic works. A feed from the sound desk or a dedicated room mic works considerably better, and it is the single biggest thing you can do to improve how well ACE follows along.

Internet, only for the optional parts — cloud detection, sermon notes, stock images, streaming, licensed Bibles, and updates. Presenting and following songs and scripture work with no internet at all.

Room on the disk. The app plus its bundled listening model is roughly a gigabyte and a half. Extra models, Bibles, and recordings grow from there. The Extras manager shows exactly what’s installed and lets you take space back.

Installing

On Mac, open the download and drag ACE Presenter onto your Applications folder, then eject the disk image. The app is signed and notarised by Apple, so it opens without a security warning.

On Windows, run the installer and follow the prompts.

Then open the app. The first launch shows a short guided tour of the dashboard; you can skip it and reopen it later from the Help menu.

Permissions

The first time you use a feature that needs it, your computer will ask permission.

Microphone — needed to follow the service or record a sermon. On Mac you’ll see a system prompt; on Windows, check Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ Microphone if you’re not asked. Audio is processed on your own computer unless you deliberately choose cloud detection.

Camera — only if you bring a camera in as a background layer.

Local network (Mac) — needed for the phone remote to find your computer. If you decline this one, the phone will sit on “Searching…” forever with no error to explain why.

If you decline something and change your mind, re-enable it in your system settings and restart ACE.

Choosing your language

ACE is available in English and Español. Go to Settings ▸ General ▸ Language, choose, and relaunch when prompted. The whole interface follows.

Section 03

Your First Service

ACE Presenter

03 · Your First Service

The shortest path from a fresh install to a screen with lyrics on it. About ten minutes.

1. Connect your second screen before opening ACE, if you can. ACE will find it either way, but starting with it connected saves a step.

2. Open ACE and set up your screens. Go to Screen Setup. You’ll see every display your computer can see. Assign the Audience output to your projector or TV. If you have a third screen for the platform, assign Stage to it. Close the dialog.

3. Add your songs. Either type them in — New Song, paste the lyrics, and ACE splits them into slides — or import what you already have. ACE reads ProPresenter, OpenLP, EasyWorship, PowerPoint, plain text, and more.

4. Build the running order. Drag songs from the library into the service plan on the left, in the order you’ll do them. Add a scripture reading by typing the reference. This list is your service.

5. Go live. Press the Live button. Your audience screen comes on. Click the first slide, and it’s on the wall.

6. Move through the service. → or Space for the next slide, ← to go back. Click any cue in the plan to jump to it.

7. Try letting it follow. Press Listening. Sing or say something from your set. Watch it find the line. Keep your hand on the arrow keys the first few times — it’s a tool, not a replacement.

That’s a service. Everything else in this manual makes it better.

Section 04

The Dashboard

Every panel on the screen, and what it controls.

ACE Presenter

04 · The Dashboard

The main window has the same shape on both platforms.

The top bar

Across the top: whether you’re live, whether ACE is listening, which venue you’re in, and the state of any extras you have running — stage screen, streaming, NDI. When something is wrong — no screen assigned, a microphone that stopped — it says so here.

The LIVE button puts your output on the wall. LISTENING starts and stops the automatic following.

Workspaces

Tabs across the top switch what the middle of the window is for:

  • Service — your running order and the slides in it. Where you spend a Sunday.
  • Library — everything you own: songs, scripture, media, templates.
  • Bible — searching and reading scripture, and putting it on screen.
  • Edit — building and changing slides.
  • Looks — the visual presets you switch between.
  • Stage — designing what the platform sees.

The panels

Left: the service plan. Your cues in order. Drag to reorder. Right-click a cue for its options.

Middle: the canvas. What is live, and what is coming next. The larger picture is what the congregation sees right now.

Right: the inspector. Whatever you’ve selected — a cue, a slide, an object. Changes here take effect immediately.

Bottom: the media tray. Images and video you’ve added. Drag one onto a cue to use it as a background.

Scenes

A row of buttons that recall an entire look — theme, layers, background — in one click. Set up “Worship”, “Sermon”, “Announcements” once, and switch between them without touching anything else. See Looks, Layers & Scenes.

Section 05

Building a Service

Assemble your run sheet — and import what you already have.

ACE Presenter

05 · Building a Service

The library

Everything you own lives here: songs, saved scripture, media, and templates. It stays between services — build it once and reuse it every week.

Adding cues

Drag from the library into the plan, or use Add Cue and choose a type:

  • Song — lyrics, split into slides
  • Scripture — a passage, by reference
  • Media — an image or video
  • Announcement — a text slide
  • Blank — a deliberate empty slide

Reorder by dragging. Remove with Delete.

Importing what you already have

Import reads:

  • ProPresenter documents
  • OpenLP and EasyWorship databases
  • PowerPoint and Keynote decks, converted page by page
  • PDF documents
  • Plain text and CSV — one song per file, or a folder at once
  • Bible XML for translations you already own

PowerPoint and PDF need a free converter. ACE uses LibreOffice to turn decks into slides. If it isn’t installed, ACE will tell you and point you at it rather than failing quietly.

Templates

Save a whole service shape — welcome, three songs, reading, sermon, closing — as a template, and start next week from it instead of a blank page.

Saving and opening

⌘S / Ctrl+S saves. ACE also saves as you work, so a crash costs you very little. Export a service to move it to another machine.

Section 06

Songs

Create, edit, arrange, and present your lyrics.

ACE Presenter

06 · Songs

Creating a song

New Song, give it a title, paste the lyrics. ACE splits them into slides on blank lines. Mark sections — Verse 1, Chorus, Bridge — and ACE labels them.

Keep one thought per slide. Two lines is usually right; four is usually too many. The congregation reads at a glance or not at all.

Arrangements

One song, several running orders. A song might be Verse 1 – Chorus – Verse 2 – Chorus, or Verse 1 – Chorus – Bridge – Chorus – Chorus. Build both, name them, and pick one when you add the song to a service. The lyrics live in one place; only the order changes.

How lyrics appear

Slides use whatever theme is active. If a line is too long, ACE shrinks it to fit rather than cutting it off. Section labels (“CHORUS”) can be shown or hidden per theme.

Section 07

Scripture & the Bible

Find, compare, study, and display any passage.

ACE Presenter

07 · Scripture & the Bible

Finding a passage

Open the Bible workspace and type a reference — John 3:16, Rom 8, 1 Cor 13:4-7. ACE understands book abbreviations, spoken numbers, and most of the ways people write references.

You can also search by words: type a phrase and ACE finds verses containing it.

Putting it on screen

Select a passage and send it live, or add it to your plan as a cue so it’s ready in order.

How much shows at once

Per theme, you can choose to show one verse at a time or a whole passage, whether verse numbers appear, whether the reference is shown, and whether the translation name is displayed.

Translations

ACE ships with nine Bibles built in and works entirely offline: English (KJV, ASV, BBE, WEB), Spanish, French, Portuguese, Arabic, and Chinese. You can import others.

Licensed translations — ESV and the API.Bible catalogue — are available on a Pro licence and served live over the internet, so no copyrighted text is stored in the app.

Comparison slides put two, three, or four translations of the same verse side by side.

Mac only. Cross-reference study — “what else speaks to this verse” — is currently Mac only. The Windows version has the underlying data and the feature is planned.

Section 08

Following the Service Automatically

ACE Presenter

08 · Following the Service Automatically

This is the part that makes ACE different, and the part worth understanding properly.

What it does

ACE listens to the room through your microphone and works out what’s happening. When the band sings a line from a song in your plan, it finds that line and moves to it. When someone says a Bible reference out loud, it finds the passage.

Starting and stopping

Press LISTENING. Press it again to stop. Nothing is recorded or sent anywhere unless you have deliberately turned on cloud detection or sermon recording.

Choosing what to follow

A three-way switch:

  • Auto — songs and scripture both. The normal setting.
  • Song — lyrics only. Useful during a long worship set.
  • Bible — scripture only. Useful during the sermon, so a sung phrase doesn’t pull the screen away.

How song-following works

ACE compares what it hears against the words of the songs in your running order — not every song you own. That’s deliberate: a smaller haystack means fewer wrong answers. If a song isn’t in the plan, ACE won’t jump to it.

It expects to move forward. If it hears a line from three slides ahead, it will go there; it is more cautious about jumping backwards, because a repeated chorus line shouldn’t send the screen back to the first verse.

How scripture-following works

Two different things happen.

A spoken reference — “turn with me to John chapter three verse sixteen” — is recognised directly and can go straight to the screen.

A quoted or paraphrased verse — the preacher says “the Lord is my shepherd, I shall not want” without giving a reference — is matched by meaning, and offered to you as suggestions rather than put on screen. You choose whether to show one. ACE never guesses a verse onto the wall.

Works differently here. Suggestions work differently on each platform, and on Mac they only run for English Bibles — ACE switches them off for other languages rather than offering confident nonsense.

Chapter-only references never fire by themselves. If someone says “John chapter three” and stops, ACE shows you the chapter as a suggestion and waits, because “verse 1” is almost never what they meant.

Reading the transcript

The Transcript panel shows what ACE actually heard, scrolling, and you can select and copy from it. When something unexpected happens, this is the first place to look — very often the app heard something different from what was said, and that explains everything.

Which listener to use

On-device (the default) runs entirely on your computer. No internet, nothing leaves the room, and it works in a church hall with no signal.

Cloud is more accurate on difficult audio but needs a steady connection and sends audio to a service to be transcribed. Choose it deliberately, not by accident.

Models

The on-device listener comes in sizes. The bundled ones handle English and most European languages. Larger models are more accurate, particularly for languages like Yoruba, Swahili, Igbo, and Zulu, and can be downloaded from Extras. Bigger models need a faster computer.

Audio input

Pick which microphone ACE listens to in Detection Settings. If you can feed it from the sound desk, do — it is the single biggest improvement available to you.

When it doesn’t follow well

In rough order of what actually helps:

  1. Improve the audio. A desk feed beats a room mic; a room mic beats a laptop. Most following problems are microphone problems.
  2. Check the transcript panel. If the words are wrong there, the fault is upstream of the matching.
  3. Make sure the song is in your plan. ACE only matches what’s in the running order.
  4. Use Bible mode during the sermon and Song mode during worship, rather than leaving it on Auto all service.
  5. Adjust sensitivity in Detection Settings, but only after the above. Two settings control when ACE bothers listening: a floor below which audio is treated as silence, and a ceiling above which loud audio always gets through.
Section 09

Song Identification & Timing

ACE Presenter

09 · Song Identification & Timing

Mac only. Both features below are currently Mac only. The Windows version identifies songs from the words that are sung, which does work; recognising a track by its sound is not there yet.

The Song Bank

ACE can recognise a song by its sound, not just its words — useful for produced tracks and original recordings. Add your own audio to the Song Bank and ACE learns to identify it.

Tap-to-learn timing

For a studio track where the slides should follow the recording exactly: right-click a song, choose Learn Timing, play the recording, and tap once at each slide change. ACE remembers the timing and follows itself on playback.

Section 10

Themes

Design every slide — type, colour, backgrounds, outlines, and motion.

ACE Presenter

10 · Themes

A theme is how your slides look — the background, the type, where the words sit. Themes are reusable: build one for lyrics, one for scripture, and switch.

The editor

Open the Theme Editor. It has three parts.

Left: your themes and slides. The themes you’ve built, and within the selected theme, the slide templates it contains. The + adds; Delete at the bottom removes.

Middle: the canvas. A live preview of a slide. Drag objects to move them, drag the handles to resize. What you see is what the congregation gets.

Right: the inspector. Changes whatever you’ve selected.

Objects

A slide is made of objects — text, shapes, images — stacked in front of the background.

The Objects list at the top of the inspector shows everything on the slide, front-most first. Each row lets you select it, hide it with the eye, or move it forward and back. This matters more than it sounds: an object dragged off the edge or sized to nothing can’t be clicked on the canvas, and the list is how you get it back.

Selecting an object shows its properties:

Content — what a text box draws. Choose Lyrics, Verse text, Reference, Translation, or Section label, and it fills itself in at service time. Or choose Custom text and type something fixed.

Typography — font, size, colour, bold, italic, underline, strikethrough, capitals, line spacing, letter spacing, and alignment both across and down the box. The + beside the font list imports a font file, so your church’s own typeface can be used even on a computer that doesn’t have it installed.

Stroke and Shadow — an outline and a drop shadow. These two matter more than they look: over a bright video background, plain white text can disappear, and an outline or shadow is what keeps it readable.

Opacity, Rotate, and Z-order — how transparent, how turned, and what’s in front of what. Back and Front move the selected object to either end of the stack.

Position — six buttons snap an object to an edge or the centre; sliders and number boxes place it exactly.

Build (entrance) — how the object arrives when the slide goes live: fade, slide in from a direction, or scale up, with a duration and a delay.

Backgrounds

With nothing selected, the inspector shows the slide and its background. Four kinds:

  • Solid — one colour.
  • Gradient — two colours and an angle.
  • Image — a picture, with a choice of how it fills the frame, and a dim slider. Dim matters: a photograph interesting enough to use is usually busy enough to make lyrics hard to read, and dimming it is the fix.
  • Alpha — nothing at all. Use this for a theme that sits over video, such as a lower third or a feed going to a broadcast switcher. On the canvas it appears as a checkerboard so you can tell “deliberately empty” from “black”.

Making a theme live

Make Active sends the theme you’re editing to the audience output. The button is red while pressing it would change what’s on screen, and green once that theme is live.

Editing aids

Rulers and guides help you line things up, and undo (⌘Z / Ctrl+Z) works throughout.

Section 11

Looks, Layers & Scenes

Compose the screen in layers and recall whole scenes in one click.

ACE Presenter

11 · Looks, Layers & Scenes

Three related ideas, easy to confuse.

Layers

Your output is built from layers, stacked:

  • Background — image or video behind everything
  • Media — a picture or clip for this cue
  • Slide — the words
  • Props — logos, lower thirds, watermarks
  • Messages — text you send during the service

Each can be cleared independently. Clearing the slide layer leaves the background running — useful during prayer, where you want the screen alive but wordless.

Looks

A look is a saved combination of layer settings — which are on, how transparent, how they blend. Switch looks to change the feel without rebuilding anything.

Scenes

A scene recalls everything at once: theme, look, layers, background. Build “Worship”, “Sermon”, “Announcements”, and move between them with a single click. This is the tool most operators end up using most.

Which to use

  • Theme — how words look. Change when the design changes.
  • Look — which layers show. Change within a service.
  • Scene — everything together. Change between parts of a service.
Section 12

Live Operator Tools

Messages, quick screens, speaker timers, and hands-free voice control.

ACE Presenter

12 · Live Operator Tools

Messages

Send text to the screen without disturbing the slide underneath — “The nursery is open”, “Please silence your phones”. Position it, choose how long it stays, and clear it.

Quick Screen

An immediate blank, a colour, or a holding slide, without touching your running order. For the moment something goes wrong and you need the screen to stop saying what it’s saying.

Timers and countdowns

Put a countdown on the stage screen — “5 minutes until service” — or a speaker timer counting the sermon. The congregation sees nothing; the platform does.

Clearing and blanking

Clear a layer to take part of the picture away. Blank to take the whole screen to black. Both are instant and both are reversible.

Voice commands

Speak to ACE while it’s listening: “next slide”, “blank the screen”, “go to song two”. Turn it on in Detection Settings. Useful when you’re not at the desk; needs a mic that can hear you over the room.

Section 14

Screens & Outputs

ACE Presenter

14 · Screens & Outputs

Screen Setup

The dialog where you say what appears where. Every display your computer can see is listed, with its size and refresh rate.

Assign each output a role:

  • Audience — what the congregation sees
  • Stage — what the platform sees: current slide, next slide, timers, notes
  • Confidence — a plain copy of the audience output
  • Lobby — a separate feed for foyer screens

Per output you can set fullscreen or windowed, adjust brightness, contrast, and saturation, apply corner-pin to square up a skewed projector, and split one image across several screens for an LED wall.

Each output also has a toggle for the audience and the stage independently, so you can take one screen out without disturbing the other.

The stage display

The platform’s view. Show current slide, next slide, a clock, a countdown, the sermon notes, or a message from you. Lay it out to suit your team — a preacher wants notes and a timer; a worship leader wants the next line.

Venues

Save a whole screen arrangement as a venue. If you set up in a hall on Sunday and a chapel on Wednesday, save both and switch — every assignment, resolution, and correction comes back.

Going live

The Live button puts your output on the wall. Until then, ACE previews everything internally, so you can build and rehearse with the projector dark.

Section 15

Audio & the Room

ACE Presenter

15 · Audio & the Room

Audio roles

ACE can send different audio to different places: the main mix to the house, a separate feed to stage monitors, translation channels to headphones. Each is assigned to a device.

Translation channels

For a multilingual service, each language can be its own channel to its own output, so an interpreter’s audio reaches only the people who need it.

Spatial audio

For anyone listening on headphones — an operator, an interpreter, a broadcast monitor — ACE can place sounds in space rather than flat in the middle of your head. A translation feed can sit to one side, the main mix in front, and the two stay separable when you’re listening to both.

Turn it on for the headphone output. It affects headphones only; the house mix is unchanged.

Section 16

Streaming & Video

ACE Presenter

16 · Streaming & Video

Bringing video in

A camera, capture device, or NDI source can be used as a background layer — the picture behind the words.

Streaming out

ACE streams your program output to YouTube, Facebook, or anywhere that takes RTMP. Enter the server and key from the platform, choose a resolution and bitrate, pick which microphone to include, and go live.

NDI

ACE can send your program out as an NDI source, so other software on the same network — a streaming machine, a switcher, a recorder — can pick it up as a video input with no cable between them. It can also take NDI in as a background layer.

NDI needs the free NDI runtime installed on the computer. If it isn’t there, ACE says so and points you at it rather than failing quietly.

Switcher control

Works differently here. On Mac, cues can drive a Blackmagic ATEM switcher. On Windows, ACE can connect to an ATEM and prove it’s reachable, but cue-driven control isn’t built yet.

Section 17

The Phone Remote

ACE Presenter

17 · The Phone Remote

Run the service from anywhere in the room.

Install ACE Presenter Remote on your phone, connect it to the same Wi-Fi, and it finds your computer by itself. From the phone you can move through slides, jump to a cue, see the running order, blank the screen, clear a layer and bring it back, and control media playback.

Useful when you’re on the platform, at the desk at the back, or moving between the two.

If the phone can’t find the computer: they must be on the same network, and on Mac the local-network permission must be granted. Without it the phone searches forever and never says why.

Section 19

Playbooks

ACE Presenter

19 · Playbooks

A Sunday service

Build the plan the week before: welcome slide, songs in order with the right arrangements, the reading, a sermon holding slide, closing song, announcements.

Set up scenes for Worship, Sermon, and Announcements.

On the day: connect screens, open the service, check Screen Setup found everything, go live on the welcome slide. Start listening as the band starts. Switch to Bible mode when the preacher stands up. Keep a hand near the arrow keys — following is an assistant, not an autopilot.

A conference

Multiple sessions, multiple speakers. Use templates for the repeating shape. The stage display with a speaker timer earns its keep here more than anywhere.

A multilingual service

Set your Bible to the congregation’s language and use comparison slides where two languages are read. Route translation audio to separate channels.

On Mac, verse suggestions run for English only — spoken references still work in every language. Explicit references are the reliable path in any language.

A service with a live band

Feed ACE from the sound desk, not a room mic. Use Song mode during worship. Expect to help it more during instrumental sections, where there are no words to hear.

Section 22

Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers to the questions operators ask most.

ACE Presenter

22 · Frequently Asked Questions

Does ACE need the internet? Not to present or to follow the service. Only optional extras need it.

Is my audio sent anywhere? Not by default. On-device listening never leaves your computer. Cloud detection and sermon notes do use the internet, and both are choices you make deliberately.

Can I use it on one screen? Yes. ACE previews every output internally, so you can build and rehearse anywhere.

Why isn’t it following the songs? Almost always the audio. Check the transcript panel: if the words are wrong there, improve the microphone before changing any setting.

Why did it put the wrong verse up? Check the transcript for what it actually heard. Spoken references are reliable; quoted verses are offered as suggestions rather than displayed.

Can two people run it at once? One computer runs the service; the phone remote is a second pair of hands on the same service.

What happens if it crashes mid-service? ACE saves as you work. Reopen and you’ll be where you were. Your outputs come back with the venue.

Section 23

When Something Goes Wrong

ACE Presenter

23 · When Something Goes Wrong

The audience screen is black. Check you’re live. Check Screen Setup has the audience output assigned to the right display and enabled. Check the screen is on the right input.

Slides look wrong on the projector but right on my screen. The theme is being scaled to a different shape. Check the projector’s resolution in Screen Setup, and use corner-pin if the projector is at an angle.

ACE isn’t hearing anything. Check the right microphone is selected in Detection Settings, that the system gave ACE permission, and that the transcript panel is moving. If the top bar says listening but the transcript is still, the microphone has stopped — stop and start listening again.

It follows for a while and then stops. Stop and restart listening. If it happens again, turn on Log detection trace and send the log — that file exists precisely for this.

The phone remote can’t find the computer. Same Wi-Fi, and on Mac the local-network permission must be granted.

PowerPoint won’t import. LibreOffice is needed for that conversion. ACE will say so and point you at it.

Everything is slow. A large listening model on a modest computer is the usual cause; switch to a smaller one in Detection Settings. Closing other applications helps more than you’d expect.
